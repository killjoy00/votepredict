# Minnesota Senate raw candidate questionnaires: 2026 publisher recovery, historical expansion and identity links

## Scope
This collector stores only literal original-publisher question/answer pairs, unchanged original source bodies and retrieval metadata. It neither judges statements nor determines legislative voting positions. The requested Oct. 16, 2026 pass targets **all 2026 candidates before their general election**, plus **only documented winners of the completed 2022 general, 2024 Senate special and 2025 Senate special elections**. No prediction, primary result, shared district or subsequent candidacy is treated as winning the original race. The existing 2020 source catalog remains unchanged for historical reference, but **2020 is excluded from the protected production replay**.

## MHP / Habitat 2026
The MHP public index explicitly enumerates Minnesota *Senate* respondent pages under `/2026_questionnaire/`. The existing 23 and five newly indexed Senate candidate pages are the only intended source pages. Individual page extraction requires real candidate identity, Senate district, and at least two printed ordered Q&As; empty publisher search results, House answers, blank submission forms and anti-bot pages are never substituted for published answers. Anonymous automated source access can legitimately fail with HTTP 403; those are reported without using credentials, proxies or access-control bypasses. Sources are not counted as captured unless their actual Q&As are stored.

## Additional 2022 and special-election sources
- **2022 Star Tribune Duluth Senate guide**: The publication explicitly says answers were **edited for length**. Raw captures are the *publisher-edited text as printed*, not full unedited candidate submissions. Only the 2022 general-election winners Grant Hauschild (SD3) and Jen McEwen (SD8); exclude their defeated opponents, State House, county candidates and explicit nonrespondents.
- **2024 Senate District 45 (MPR)**: Only special-election winner Ann Johnson Stewart, never Kathleen Fowke. Publisher guide describes a three-question survey, but no records are written unless all required question/answer text is actually machine-readable. Introductory candidate biography or embedded/interactive loading text is insufficient.
- **2025 Senate District 60 DFL special election**: The original publicly linked Doron Clark endorsement questionnaire PDF is in scope because he won the final special election. Four other identified primary/endorsement respondents did not win the special election and their URLs are excluded from the production catalog. Attempt anonymous original file download using Google Drive's standard public-file endpoint. Only a genuine, text-selectable PDF with configured candidate name, district/endorsement context and 2+ legible ordered Q&As is eligible. Retain the **original complete PDF bytes, base64-encoded losslessly in `raw_content`**, and hash the exact original PDF bytes. Label `content_type=application/pdf;base64`; never misrepresent PDF as HTML. Sign-in requirements, non-PDF responses or image-only/unreadable source bodies are reported as unavailable, not bypassed.

## Person linkage
Reuse `legislators`, `memberships`, `membership_source_aliases` and the existing shared `resolveSenator` session/district/name resolver. A unique sitting senator can be joined; a challenger who has never served in that session remains unmatched, even when only one sitting senator represents the same district. November 2022 Senate candidates are compared with **2023–2024** service memberships (not 2021–2022); 2024 special-election questionnaires with **2023–2024**; 2025/2026 with **2025–2026**. The protected collector performs a bounded **null-only** reconciliation of only eligible questionnaire links, updates neither candidate answers nor other raw sources, and reports its match count. Historical nonwinners are never linked by the new backfill.

All collection/identity writes use the existing protected default Neon project and existing GitHub Actions independently reviewed-main gate. Existing raw Q&A snapshots, duplicate history and published dates are immutable. No changes to serving forecasts, feature tables, model weights or prediction logic.

## Outcome provenance and legacy scope
Election-winner eligibility is strictly keyed to the original race cycle, Senate district and the official general/special election result, **not a 2026 forecast**. The bounded source catalog includes five 2022 winners (Grant Hauschild, Jen McEwen, Bill Weber, Gene Dornink, Steve Cwodzinski), 2024 special-election winner Ann Johnson Stewart and 2025 special-election winner Doron Clark. Actual original result sources:

- 2022 Secretary of State Senate district results: https://electionresults.sos.mn.gov/Results/Index?ersElectionId=149&scenario=StateSenate
- 2024 Secretary of State Senate District 45: https://electionresults.sos.mn.gov/Results/Index?ersElectionId=170&scenario=StateSenate
- 2025 Secretary of State Senate District 60: https://electionresults.sos.mn.gov/results/Index?DistrictId=548&ErsElectionId=178&scenario=StateSenate&show=Go

The 2022 Bio-Fuels article has a documented **“Billl Weber”** typographical error in its respondent heading but identifies **Bill Weber** in its introduction (https://mnbiofuels.org/candidates-biofuels-weber-dornink). Eligibility and membership linkage recognize that single exact spelling only for the same source URL, 2022 cycle and Senate District 21; the stored raw candidate name is **not** rewritten.

The completed 2022 Bio-Fuels loser-only article URLs and four unsuccessful SD60 questionnaire links are intentionally no longer fetched. Articles containing both winners and nonwinners are filtered **before** `onResponse`/database capture; a second guard refuses out-of-scope direct inserts. A historical candidate outside this verified, bounded winner set is omitted pending election-outcome evidence, not guessed into the winner group. All 2026 respondents remain eligible irrespective of incumbency, party or prospective election outcome.

Some 2022 losing-candidate raw captures were stored **before** this scope clarification. Those physical rows and their exact published text are not deleted or rewritten: they remain immutable source history. The protected production report now separately shows the in-scope 2026+historical winner counts versus legacy historical loser/unknown captures. **Only the in-scope candidate set should be used for subsequent modeling or research**; the raw capture table's unrestricted physical row count is not itself an eligible modeling cohort. Neither existing forecasts nor model weights are changed in this work.

## 2026 public policy expansion — original Education Minnesota forms

The original <https://www.edmnvotes.org/voter-guide/> explicitly links to completed 2026 State **Senate** screening PDFs under the Minnesota Senate subsection. This pass pins **18 cycle-eligible original candidate PDF URLs** (SD02, SD03, SD08, SD09, SD13, SD14, SD17, SD18, SD26, SD29, SD31, SD32, SD48, SD52, SD55, SD57, SD62, SD67). The full original PDF bytes and binary SHA-256 are kept with each append-only capture; its printable questions and answers are extracted without subjective interpretation. These questionnaires cover school funding and higher education, revenue/tax policy, collective bargaining and paid leave, educator pension and health insurance costs, and worksite and school safety. SNAP and the earlier KAXE publisher responses remain the complementary energy/environment and broader cost-of-living sources; this addition is **not** proof that every named policy area has equivalent coverage or both-party representation.

The PDF forms place many Yes/No responses *before* the question in text extraction and place narrative responses before the question on other pages. Only explicitly printed values adjacent to their numbered questions are retained, in document order. A missing mark or blank answer does not imply opposition, neutrality or refusal. PDF links must resolve to the original publisher host, the document must name the configured candidate and print a matching Senate district in the footer, and at least 15 distinct unambiguous Q&As must be extracted. PDF headers sometimes reorder a zero-padded district in text extraction, so the independent repeated candidate/district footer is used to check against the published Senate-index link. Any mismatch or unreadable page is a reported failure; do not substitute a candidate bio or an organization endorsement for a questionnaire answer.

### Conservative cross-publisher candidate grouping

`senateQuestionnaireCandidateGroupReadback` provides a **read-only**, explicit capture-to-candidate grouping from cycle, **full printed name**, district, publisher, and previously verified membership links. It treats known single-middle-initial variants (Dan R. Rogers / Dan Rogers in SD2; Krystyne M. Knutson / Krystyne Knutson in SD9) as the same candidate only in the 2026 race/district where original publishers print those variants. Different districts, unknown districts, contradictory parties, and conflicting previously linked member identities are not collapsed. The resulting candidate-only group is **not** an incumbent/member link; the independent exact-or-verified-alias sitting-senator resolver retains responsibility for that link. Cross-publisher group keys are derived in the reusable operation and included in protected production readback without creating a new registry, modifying original captures, inventing a winner or editing historical 2022 losers.

Raw capture count, distinct candidate groups and multi-publisher joins are reported separately; neither collector count nor the fact that a publisher endorses a candidate implies a legislative policy position or vote.

### Publisher cycle mismatch and malformed PDF (Oct. 16 follow-up)
The publisher's 2026 voter-guide index linked Simon Glaser's District 24 PDF under the current candidate listing, but the **PDF itself explicitly says 2025 Fall Special Election**, not 2026. It must never be imported or relabeled as a 2026 candidate questionnaire. The previously cataloged URL has therefore been removed from the current-cycle source catalog; no capture was created from it.

Chris Ford's Senate District 32 form is a real, text-selectable **2026** candidate screening PDF. PDF.js can reject its literal source bytes with `unsupported Unicode escape sequence`. For that specific parser failure only, the collector attempts bounded local Poppler `pdftotext -layout` on the very same fetched original bytes, then reruns the unchanged name/district and Q&A completeness guards. It never uses excerpts, a secondary document or offsite conversion, and continues to store the original unmodified PDF bytes with their exact binary hash. The extra live source smoke explicitly requires a complete parsed Chris Ford source before merge. If both text extractors fail, mark the source unavailable instead of inserting invented answers.

### Original PDF encoding guard for PostgreSQL JSONB (Oct. 16)
The protected replay of the District 32 source exposed an additional **database-only encoding failure** after local PDF extraction succeeded: PostgreSQL rejects a `\\u0000` Unicode escape in the ordered Q&A JSONB content-aware deduplication comparison. The binary PDF remains unchanged and losslessly stored as base64 with its original SHA-256. Only decoded *question/answer text* is converted to valid UTF-8 scalars by substituting U+FFFD for the non-storable U+0000 or an unpaired UTF-16 surrogate, leaving all valid Unicode and published words untouched. The publisher-original PDF remains available for exact-byte recovery. A disposable PostgreSQL `$1::jsonb` roundtrip now uses the live Chris Ford source response to block a repeat Neon import failure; no scoring, inference, model behavior, or existing snapshots are modified.
