# Issue #864 — 109 exact senator campaign issue excerpts with VERIFIED original archive capture dates

## The practical date rule

**We do not need to know when a campaign webpage was first authored.** When an independently authenticated actual Wayback original HTML response contains a candidate-owned policy statement, we attribute it as **publicly observable by the exact UTC capture timestamp** proven from that original source. The archive timestamp is a genuine dated research observation, not an invented date of first writing or a 2026 retrieval. Earlier independently captured original copies can establish an *earlier* public-by timestamp when the **identical exact text** reappears; the auditor must re-open the earlier original source page and prove it separately.

A dated, original campaign-site excerpt is a **candidate-authored site statement** for research attribution. It is NOT automatically a verified legislator roll-call vote, an official contemporaneous SOS filed-candidate website URL, or an approved past-date forecast feature. Historical primary-vs-general election cutoffs must be compared against the **actual archive date**, rather than silently assuming content was available earlier.

The [source fixture](../data/evaluation/senate-capture-dated-original-2020-22-campaign-issue-excerpts-v1.json) pins **109 distinct, manually selected short, exact, personally attributable candidate-owned site policy excerpts** across **12 actual Minnesota State Senate-serving people** from **14 original full candidate-owned page captures** (12 captured during 2022 and two during the earlier 2019–20 campaign research window). This creates 12 additional original page-sourced people compared with earlier research for ten different serving senators; neither corpus is claimed exhaustive.

### Independent real-original verification (not merely trusting a copied quote)

The [offline runner](../scripts/audit-senate-109-capture-dated-campaign-excerpts-offline.ts) downloads **no external data** itself. Its PR-only [source-workflow](../.github/workflows/audit-109-senator-2020-22-capture-dated-policy-quotes.yml) obtains the original public official Minnesota LRL Senate-service directory HTML (source run **38094976711**) and both first-party historical archived campaign HTML/CDX original bundles (2022 **38107693123**; 2020 **38107731662**). Before accepting **any** statement, it reopens and independently SHA-256 + historical CDX-SHA1 authenticates **all 68 source pages** using the previously green source auditor from [#1016](https://github.com/killjoy00/votepredict/pull/1016), checks each candidate's actual official Senate service and source URL/time, and then physically rechecks all **109 exact visible excerpts** in their candidate-owned page's issue/priorities/content section. Seven Gary and two Mary statements must separately match earlier genuine originals before receiving an earlier public-by date.

A page merely carrying an issue category in site navigation, a testimonial by a third party, an empty page, a candidate filing name or a post-election result is NOT sufficient. Where a 2022 original documents earlier-cycle copy or prior legislative work, the ledger explicitly preserves that context and **does not** invent a fresh 2022 author date or individual Senate vote.

**Important distinctions:** The archive capture is the attribute date; absence from a bounded archived page sample is not proof of a senator's lack of a position. No public-office outreach or user database authorization was requested. No actual evidence row insertion, SELECT/UPDATE against Neon, Vercel deployment, production metadata change, model training, evaluation freeze modifications, forecast feature admission, scheduler or 2027 work.

The 109 new statements are research **leads** to carry forward into later source-to-evidence completeness work, not 109 new recorded production issue-position documents.
